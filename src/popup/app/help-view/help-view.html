How to use it

  1. Press Start and work on one thing until the timer ends.
  2. Take the short break. Step away from the screen if you can.
  3. After 4 focus sessions (you can change this), take a long break.

The bar under the clock empties as time runs out, and the dots at the bottom count focus sessions until your long break.

Settle in

Optional. Turn on Settle in before focus under Focus helpers in Settings. Pressing Start then begins with 30 seconds of slow breathing: the bar fills as you breathe in and empties as you breathe out, a small number counts down each breath, and the clock waits. Start now skips it. Prefer something rounder? Turn on Breathing circle just below it, and a soft circle grows and shrinks with each breath in place of the clock.

First tiny step

Optional. Turn on Ask for the first tiny step under Focus helpers in Settings. Pressing Start on a new focus session asks what the very first step is, like "open the doc". Small and specific makes it easier to begin. Your answer shows under the timer until the session ends, and Skip and start goes straight in.

Where was I?

Optional. Turn on Where was I? under Focus helpers in Settings. When you pause a focus session, or on the break after one, a card asks where you left off, like "halfway through section 2". Your note shows under the timer when you come back, so getting back in takes seconds, and it clears itself once the next focus session is done. It stays on this device.

Step away

Optional, in the browser extension. Turn on Pause when I step away under Focus helpers in Settings. If the computer sits untouched for 5 minutes during focus, the timer pauses from the moment you left, so wandering off doesn't quietly use up the session. When you're back, a notification asks: Resume where you left off, or I was working if you were reading or thinking, and the time counts.

Small wins

Optional. Turn on Celebrate small wins under Focus helpers in Settings. When a focus session finishes, the progress bar glows and a quick line like "Nice work! 3 done today." shows for a few seconds. If the timer was closed, it celebrates the next time you open it on that break. No streaks and nothing to lose.

Focus garden

Optional. Turn on Focus garden under Focus helpers in Settings. Stats then shows this week's garden, with a little plant for each pomodoro you finish, Monday to Sunday. A fresh garden starts every Monday, and nothing ever wilts.

Energy check-in

Optional. Turn on Energy check-in under Focus helpers in Settings. On the break after a focus session, tap Low, Okay or Great for how it went, or ignore it. Stats gets a Time of day section that shows how mornings, afternoons, evenings and nights tend to go, once each has a few check-ins. It stays on this device like the rest of your stats.

Distraction tally

Optional. Turn on Distraction tally under Focus helpers in Settings. During focus, tap I drifted next to the status line whenever you notice your mind wandered, then come right back. Stats shows this week's drifts next to last week's. Noticing is the skill being trained, so a tap is a small win, not a mark against you.

Weekly look-back

Optional. Turn on Weekly look-back under Focus helpers in Settings. The first time you open the timer each week, a short card shows last week's pomodoros, the time you focused, and your best day. Tap Nice and it's gone until next week.

Take-care reminders

Optional. Turn on Take-care reminders under Focus helpers in Settings. After every 3 focus sessions in a row, that break suggests one small thing for you: a glass of water, resting your eyes, a stretch or a snack. Sessions count as in a row while the gaps between them are no longer than a long break plus 15 minutes.

Just start

Optional. Turn on Offer a 10-minute start under Timers in Settings. On days when 25 minutes feels like too much, tap or just start: 10 min under the clock. Getting going is the hard part, and 10 minutes is easier to say yes to. It counts as a pomodoro like any other.

Rhythm presets

Optional. Turn on Rhythm presets on the timer under Timers in Settings. Before a focus session starts, tap Classic (25/5), Short (15/3) or Deep (50/10) under the timer choices. It sets your focus, short break and long break lengths in one go, and you can still fine-tune them in Settings.

Heads-up

Optional. Turn on Heads-up a minute before under When time's up in Settings. In the last minute of focus or a break, the progress bar glows softly and the timer says one minute is left. With sound on, you'll hear a soft tone too. Switching gears is easier when it isn't a surprise.

Paused reminder

Optional. Turn on Remind me when paused under When time's up in Settings. If the timer sits paused for 10 minutes, a quiet notification asks whether to pick it back up or start over, so a pause for a quick question doesn't quietly eat the afternoon.

Hourly time check

Optional. Turn on Hourly time check under Focus helpers in Settings. On the hour, from 8 am to 9 pm, a quiet notification says what time it is, like "It's 3 PM", and how much time a running timer has left. It's a gentle nudge for days when time slips away, timer or not.

Calm clock

Optional. Turn on Calm clock under Appearance in Settings. The timer shows whole minutes ("24 min") instead of counting down the seconds, and the progress bar still shows how far along you are. Some people find a ticking clock makes them watch the time instead of doing the work.

Visual timer

Optional. Turn on Visual timer under Appearance in Settings. A colored disc takes the place of the numbers and shrinks as time passes, like a kitchen timer. Many people find "about half left" easier to feel than "12:37".

Color themes

Optional. Under Appearance in Settings, pick Colors: Classic (orange, green and blue), Forest, Ocean, Sunset or Lavender. Each one gives focus, short breaks and long breaks their own color, on the timer, in Stats and on the toolbar badge. Color-blind friendly colors and high contrast, under Accessibility, take over when they're on.

Live toolbar icon

Optional, in the browser extension. Turn on Live toolbar icon under Appearance in Settings. While the timer runs, the Tomomomento icon in your toolbar becomes a ring in the phase's color that empties as time passes, so you can see roughly how much is left without opening anything. Hover over it for the exact minutes.

Big timer view

Optional. Turn on Big timer view under Appearance in Settings, then press the expand button at the top of the timer. The clock fills the whole window, with the phase's color sinking down the screen as time passes, so you can read it from across the room. In the browser extension it opens in its own tab. Press Space to start or pause, and Escape or the close button to go back. For true full screen, press F11 too.

Ends at

Optional. Turn on Show when it ends under Appearance in Settings. While focus or a break runs, a small line under the timer says the time it ends, like "Ends at 2:45 PM". It's handy when time slips away, or when you're planning around a meeting.

Keyboard shortcuts

Optional. Turn on Keyboard shortcuts under Accessibility in Settings. On the timer, press S to skip to the next timer, R to restart this one, P to park a thought, and D to note a drift. Space starts or pauses whether they're on or not. They're off by default so a stray key press never changes anything.

Animated digits

Optional. Turn on Animated digits under Appearance in Settings. Each number on the clock rolls gently into place as it changes. If your device is set to reduce motion, the digits stay still.

Lava lamp

Optional. Turn on Lava lamp background under Appearance in Settings. Soft blobs of color drift slowly behind everything, warm during focus and cool on breaks. They stay faint so all the text stays easy to read, hold still if you've asked for reduced motion, and are left out in high contrast.

Minimal timer

Optional. Turn on Minimal timer under Appearance in Settings. The timer shows just the clock, the progress bar and one button, with a bigger clock and nothing else asking for attention. Tap Show more for everything else. It tidies itself back up when the timer starts or you open it again.

Accessibility

Settings has an Accessibility section. Text size makes everything bigger. High contrast darkens text and strengthens colors and edges. Color-blind friendly colors make focus orange, short breaks blue and long breaks purple, which stay distinct with every common kind of color blindness. The easy-read font is Atkinson Hyperlegible, made so similar letters and numbers can't be confused. Reduce motion turns off animations.

Focus sound

Optional. Pick Soft ticking, Brown noise, Rain or Ocean waves under Focus sound in Settings, and set its volume there. It plays quietly while focus runs and stops for breaks and pauses. The ticks fall in step with the clock's seconds, brown noise is a deep, steady hush like a distant waterfall, rain patters softly, and the waves roll in and wash out about every 8 seconds.

Time's up sounds

Optional. Turn on Play a sound under When time's up in Settings, then pick one: a bell, a chime, a digital beep, a warm marimba, birdsong or a soft gong. Press the play button beside it to hear it first.

Keep going

Optional. Turn on Offer to keep going under When time's up in Settings. If focus ends while you're in the flow, tap +5 min focus at the bottom of the timer for a little more time. Your break waits for you, and you can do it again when the extra time runs out.

Stats

The chart button at the top shows how many pomodoros you've finished today, over the last 7 days, this month, and in total. Below that are a bar chart of the last week and a calendar of the last 16 weeks, where darker squares mean more pomodoros. Stats stay on this device, and Reset stats clears them.

Daily intention

Optional. Turn on Daily intention under Planning in Settings. The first time you open the timer each day, it asks "What matters most today?" Your answer stays at the top of the timer until midnight, as a quiet reminder of what the pomodoros are for. Change lets you rewrite it, and Not today skips a day. It stays on this device.

Plan my day

Optional. Turn it on under Planning in Settings.

  1. Choose how many pomodoros you'd like to do today. You'll see your focus and break time laid out, and about when you'd be done.
  2. If you like, name each one (like "Email" or "Draft report"). The timer shows the one you're on. You can skip this.
  3. The strip under the timer fills in as you go. Tap it to change your plan any time.

Each plan starts fresh the next day, and nothing is kept about plans you didn't finish. Pomodoros past your plan count as a bonus.

Parking lot

Optional. Turn it on under Planning in Settings. When a stray thought pops up mid-focus ("call the dentist"), tap + Park a thought under the timer, type it, and press Enter. It's out of your head and safe, so you can get back to work. The notepad button at the top shows everything you've parked.

On your next break, the timer lets you know what you parked. Tap the check mark next to a thought when you've dealt with it. Thoughts stay on this device until you clear them.

Day wrap-up

Optional. With the parking lot on, turn on Wrap up my day under Planning in Settings. After 5 pm, if anything is still parked, a card on the timer offers to wrap up. You'll see one thought at a time and choose Done, Keep for tomorrow or Let go. Cleared thoughts are gone for good, and nothing keeps track of which you picked. "Not tonight" hides the card until tomorrow evening.

Right-click shortcuts

Optional, in the browser extension. Turn on Right-click shortcuts under Focus helpers in Settings. With the parking lot on, select some text on any page, right-click it and choose Park this thought. With the site blocker on, right-click a page and choose Block this site during focus. A check mark flashes on the toolbar icon when it's done.

Break ideas

Optional. Turn on Suggest break ideas under Appearance in Settings, or from the card on the timer. Each break shows something quick and screen-free to do, like stretching or refilling your water, in place of "Take a breather". Not feeling it? Tap the arrow beside it for another idea.

Starter setups

Not sure where to begin? At the top of Settings, one tap on a starter setup turns on a few options that work well together: Gentle start for days when starting is hard, Deep work for long quiet stretches, Low stimulation for less on screen, and Know yourself for noticing what helps. Undo puts things back, and you can change any single option afterwards.

Finding a setting

Type in the search box at the top of Settings, like "sound" or "break", and only the settings that mention it stay on screen. Clear the box, or press Esc, to see them all again.

Back up and restore

Everything stays on this device, so keep a copy before reinstalling or switching computers. In Settings, Back up saves a file with your settings, stats, plan and parked thoughts. Restore reads that file here or on another device. Your settings are replaced, and the backup's stats and parked thoughts are added to yours, so nothing is lost.

The Pomodoro Technique®

What it is

A simple way to manage time: work on one task for 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break. Each 25-minute stretch is one "pomodoro". After four, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

Who invented it

Francesco Cirillo, as a university student in Italy in the late 1980s. Struggling to concentrate, he challenged himself to study for just 10 minutes, timed with a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. Pomodoro is Italian for tomato.

Why it works

  • Small starts beat procrastination. Committing to 25 minutes is much easier than facing a whole project.
  • One thing at a time. A pomodoro is for a single task. If something else comes up, jot it down and come back to it later.
  • A plan makes starting easier. The original technique begins each day by choosing what to work on and estimating how many pomodoros it will take.
  • Breaks keep you fresh. Regular short rests stop fatigue from building up, so your focus lasts all day.
  • Time becomes a helper, not a threat. The goal isn't to race the clock, it's to make steady progress and see how much a few focused pomodoros add up to.
